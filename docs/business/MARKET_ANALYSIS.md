# UniFed Nigeria — Market Analysis

> Directional market context for Nigeria + Sub-Saharan Africa. **No primary TAM study was
> commissioned; figures are indicative planning context, clearly labeled.** Product status is factual
> (see `COVERAGE.md`).

## 1. Market shape (Nigeria, indicative)
- **~200+ tertiary institutions** (federal, state, private universities, polytechnics, colleges).
- Federal universities alone: ~50, each 10k–80k students.
- Total tertiary enrolment: **~2–3 million students** (indicative).
- Most institutions run **fragmented portals + WhatsApp + Moodle** with poor integration/UX.

## 2. Digital transformation pressure
- NUC / ministry pushes for digitized records, accountability, graduate tracing.
- NDPA 2023 raises the bar for data-handling → favors compliant, sovereign platforms.
- "Digital Public Infrastructure" (DPI) agenda creates grant + govt-contract openings.

## 3. Willingness & ability to pay
- Public universities: budget-constrained, slow procurement, FX-sensitive → prefer grants/ivar.
- Private universities: faster decisions, higher fees, better fit for paid SaaS.
- Pilot/free entry then paid conversion is the realistic motion (see `PRICING_STRATEGY.md`).

## 4. Sub-Saharan adjacency
- Ghana, Kenya, Uganda, South Africa have analogous fragmented HE markets.
- ActivityPub federation is region-agnostic → exportable once Nigerian reference is proven.
- Localization (language, currency, compliance) required per market.

## 5. Demand drivers
- Youth bulge + mobile-first adoption.
- Employer demand for verified graduate credentials (fraud reduction).
- Research collaboration across institutions (currently manual).
- Pandemic-accelerated remote/hybrid learning expectations.

## 6. Adoption barriers
- Long procurement + ICT/SSO integration timelines (1–3 months per institution).
- Change resistance from incumbent portal vendors / internal IT.
- FX volatility on USD-priced infra.
- Connectivity gaps in rural campuses (CDN + offline-tolerant design mitigates).

## 7. Go-to-market hypothesis
1. ADUN pilot (free) → reference + case study.
2. Federated referrals to peer universities (network effect lowers CAC).
3. Grants/accelerators for credibility + non-dilutive capital.
4. Private universities as first paid; public via grants/ministry frameworks.

## 8. Market risk
- TAM is real but **untested willingness-to-pay at scale**; pilot is the de-risking event.
- Competition is *inertia*, not a single rival — slower but stubborn.

## Bottom line
A large, underserved, sovereignty-sensitive HE market with strong grant alignment and a clear
federation differentiation. The risk is execution + procurement speed, not market absence.
